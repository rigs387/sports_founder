The Spreading Heatmap

A build brief for the world map: the screen the whole game sits on, and the first thing in this project a person will be able to play rather than measure.

Drafted 2026-09-22 Branch: phase0/world-map Follows: 355cc71

The question this step answers

Prove the core fantasy is fun before investing in the most expensive systems. Phase 0 answers “is the spreading heatmap fun?” in under a year. Technical & Production Plan, §2.1

Nine steps of balance work have proved the design space is not degenerate: pacing lands on budget, no anchor is safe, rivals persist, the tree is a real decision, and contrasting genomes catch on in different halves of the world. None of that is evidence of fun, and none of it can become evidence of fun, because nobody has seen the sport spread. The map is what makes the question askable.

The GDD is unambiguous about its place: “The world map is the visual and interactive center of the game” — home, always underneath, with every other screen overlaying it rather than replacing it.

The build

  1. Market-to-shape table

    The hard part, and the part worth doing first because everything else waits on it. Each of the 213 ids in content/countries.yaml needs geometry, and the ids follow sports-body convention rather than any single cartographic source: england, scotland, wales, northern-ireland, chinese-taipei, hong-kong, republic-of-ireland.

    The table is content, not code: a checked-in file mapping each market id to the Natural Earth feature (or features) that draw it, hand-resolved once and then guarded by a test.

  2. Build-time geometry script

    scripts/build-map.ts: take the Natural Earth source, simplify with mapshaper to a size budget, project, keep only the shapes the table references, and emit one committed artifact under assets/map/. Committed rather than fetched, so a build never needs the network and the app never loads remote content.

  3. A test that the table is total

    Every market has geometry; every geometry belongs to a market or is explicitly marked unclaimed; no market is silently missing. Content validation already fails on a missing source citation, and the same standard applies here — an invisible market is a market the player can never click.

  4. The map itself

    PixiJS canvas inside React, filled shapes, pan and zoom via pixi-viewport, hover readout and click-to-select. Land outside any market drawn in neutral hatching per the GDD, belonging to nobody.

  5. The heat

    Fill each market by how far the sport has caught on there, repainted once per turn from the snapshot. This is the screen’s whole thesis: the player should watch the colour bleed outward from the anchor across a campaign.

  6. Click-through to a country

    A slide-out panel over the map — fans, league, rivals — populated from the snapshot. A plain panel is enough here; the designed version belongs to the screen-shell step.

Known trap — verify before writing the script

The planned world-atlas package ships Natural Earth admin-0 countries, where the United Kingdom is a single feature. This game has England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland as four separate markets, so admin-0 alone cannot draw them. The home nations live in Natural Earth’s map units layer, which means sourcing that layer and processing it with mapshaper rather than taking world-atlas off the shelf. Confirm this first: it decides the whole data pipeline, and getting it wrong is discovered late and painfully.

Second trap — the signature hard anchor

Tuvalu has 9,646 people and is the anchor the balance criteria single out as the hard start. At world scale it is a speck a few pixels across, and several other markets are worse. Microstates need a minimum hit target and a visible marker, or the game’s most demanding opening is on a country the player cannot point at.

Rules this branch inherits

  • The sim stays pure. Map code lives in src/renderer and imports nothing from it; npm run check:purity enforces the boundary.
  • No remote content, ever. No CDN, no remote fonts, no map tiles fetched at runtime. Geometry ships with the app.
  • Names come from content, strings from i18next. Country labels resolve through names.yaml — the one moddable names file — never hardcoded, never concatenated.
  • Tooltips explain, they do not expose formulas. “Growing fast: your focus here, strong climate fit, Street Courts”, not a lever breakdown.
  • Pinned versions. Exact pins, no upgrades of anything already installed.

Done means

  • All 213 markets render, are hoverable and are clickable, including the microstates.
  • Land belonging to no market is drawn as neutral hatching.
  • Colour tracks the sport’s spread and repaints once per turn.
  • Pan and zoom stay smooth on the full map.
  • Clicking a market opens a panel with that market’s real numbers.
  • npm run check green: types, Biome, purity, tests.
  • npm run smoke produces a screenshot of a campaign with visible spread — the first picture of this game that has ever existed.

Decisions I need from you

  1. What the colour means

    Options: share of a country’s population following the sport; Fandom Score per person; or raw Fandom Score, which would make big countries glow whatever their actual penetration.

    Recommended: share of population — the balance work already ranks countries this way, because it shows where the sport caught on rather than which country is largest.

  2. How rivals appear on the map

    Rival escalation and countermoves are per-country and already in the snapshot. They can share the fill (hue for the leader, intensity for strength), sit as a separate mark (an outline or corner flag on contested markets), or stay off the map for now.

    Recommended: a separate mark. One quantity per visual channel; the fill belongs to the player’s sport, and a contested market is a different fact about a country than how far it has fallen.

  3. Projection

    Equirectangular is trivial and looks like a spreadsheet of the world. Natural Earth is the standard compromise and is what the boundary data is drawn for. Equal-area would size markets by real area, which is honest but unfamiliar.

    Recommended: the Natural Earth projection — it matches the source data and the stylised, illustrated direction the GDD asks for.

  4. How far this branch goes

    It could stop at a working, unstyled map, or carry through to the art direction — the almanac-with-a-wink look the GDD describes.

    Recommended: stop at working. Art direction deserves its own pass with the screen shell around it, and a map you can click is what unblocks everything else.

Not in this step

Event cards and decision cards; the growth tree screen; the sport and rulebook page; the almanac; the league table; turn recap animation; any art direction pass. The map has to exist before any of them has a surface to sit on.
